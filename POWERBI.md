# Getting the validated complaints into the QC Data Tracker (PowerBI)

Chadia's requirement: the validated complaints must "funnel into PowerBI for the QC
Data Tracker", and "tie back the validated complaints to date and SKU most
importantly".

## What exists now (2026-10-19)

`automation_philip` on db01-master holds the data and has two table-shaped views at
the grains she reports on — the complaint rollup, and (since 2026-10-19) the same
rollup with the seller's sales denominator attached:

| object | grain | columns |
|---|---|---|
| `v_ai_complaints_monthly` | SKU × month × complaint | `sku, asin, parent, product, title, title_short, month (YYYY-MM), quarter, complaint, category, n, pct_of_sku_month, n_human_validated, n_repeat` |
| `v_complaint_rates` | SKU × month × complaint | the same rows plus `orders, units, refunds, complaints_per_100_orders, complaints_per_1000_units, refund_rate_pct` (see "Return rate" below) |
| `v_ai_complaints` | complaint record | `review_id, review_date, sku, asin, parent, product, title, country, source, order_id, ai_complaint, ai_category, final_complaint, final_category, human_validated, human_decision, has_prose, dup_rank, dup_group` |

`complaint` / `category` in the monthly view are the **validated** labels (the
team's correction where one exists, the AI's label otherwise). `n_human_validated`
says how many of the `n` rows a person has actually checked, so a report can show
confidence rather than implying every row was audited.

### Some seller SKUs are numeric — use `title_short`, do not "fix" the SKU

The seller's own SKU for several products really is a number: `6` is the Flux Phenom
magnetic screen door (B01ESSA9VO), `1` is the Repel umbrella (B0160HYB8S), `7` is the
Everlasting Comfort lumbar pillow (B01IJNJAZ0), `11` is the French press (B01J4O0T4E).
These are the SKU values in `amzmanager.all_products` — the titles carried on those
SKUs in the view (screen door, umbrella, lumbar pillow, French press) confirm it —
so they are not a parsing failure and must NOT be backfilled with a product code.

They read badly in a pivot ("6"), so the monthly view carries `title_short`: the head
of the product title, identical for every month of a SKU (e.g. *Flux Phenom The
Original Magnetic Screen Door*). Put `title_short` on the axis and `sku` in the
tooltip. `sku` itself is never rewritten.

## What PowerBI does with it

1. New connection: MySQL, db01-master.amzmanager.net, database `automation_philip`,
   read-only user.
2. One table import: `v_ai_complaints_monthly`.
3. Model: `month` → date table; `sku`, `parent`, `complaint`, `category` dimensions.
4. Measures: `SUM(n)`, `SUM(n_human_validated)/SUM(n)` as "share validated",
   `pct_of_sku_month` already computed per SKU-month.
5. Two visuals answer her question directly: complaints by month (stacked by
   `complaint`) and complaints by SKU for a chosen month, with `n` on the label so
   a one-review cell is not read as a trend.

### Repeat grain — read this before quoting a total

The same comment is recorded more than once on the same ORDER (`order_id` + MD5(text)): 2,050 groups covering 4,947 rows, so 2,897 rows (13.0% of the corpus) repeat a comment already recorded on that order. Nothing is deduped — every count above is every row.

The flag rides in the views as `dup_rank` / `dup_group` (rank 1 is the first row of its group, rank 2 repeats one; the ~40 rows with no `order_id` fall back to SKU + date + MD5). So:

- label `n` as **complaint records**, never "customers" or "complaints received";
- treat absolute totals as an upper bound; shares (%) are much less affected because the repeats carry the same labels;
- for distinct complaints, filter `dup_rank = 1` (the app's 'distinct complaints' toggle does the same: `dup=1`), and say so on the page — 21,000 rows and 20,000 rows are different denominators, not a restatement.

Separately, **351 rows are process noise** (`Review Deletion`: empty or non-complaint text removed by the rule). They are excluded from every distribution and are a different thing from the repeats above — do not describe them as duplicates.

Any trend before June 2026 rests on very few rows — filter `month >= '2026-06'` in any visual that claims a trend.

## Return rate: the denominator, the join, and what NULL means

**Updated 2026-10-19.** The complaint data had no denominator, so every figure was
a count of records and "returns went up" was unanswerable. The seller's own sales
feed is on the same server, and it is now the denominator.

| object | grain | columns |
|---|---|---|
| `sales_by_sku_month` (table) | seller SKU × month | `sku, month (YYYY-MM), units, orders, refunds, sales, units_us, orders_us, refunds_us, sales_us, n_countries` |
| `v_complaint_rates` (view) | SKU × month × complaint — **the same rows and the same counts as `v_ai_complaints_monthly`** | that view's columns (`n` renamed `n_complaints`) plus `orders, units, refunds, complaints_per_100_orders, complaints_per_1000_units, refund_rate_pct, refund_rate_us_pct, orders_us, units_us, refunds_us, has_sales` |

### The join, proved rather than assumed

```
amzmanager.finance_by_products.product_id  ->  amzmanager.products.id      (products IS all_products; all_products is a view over products)
amzmanager.products.sku                    ->  automation_philip.classifications.sku
```

`finance_by_products.product_id` carries no SKU of its own, so the key is
`products.id`, not `products.sku`. Measured on 2026-10-19: **184 of 184** complaint
SKUs resolve to `products.sku` — **100.00% of the 22,228 records**, including the
numeric SKUs `6`, `7`, `1`, `11`. Matching on `fnsku` instead would have covered
0.20% of records and matching on `asin` 100%, but the SKU is the seller's own key
and is the one the complaints and the finance rows already share.

At the reporting grain the coverage is **632 of 645 complaint SKU-months (97.98%)
and 21,819 of 21,833 complaint records (99.94%)**. The 13 uncovered SKU-months are
mostly bundle (`-BDL`) and one-off SKUs that have no finance row at all; they show
NULL, never 0.

### Grain

`sales_by_sku_month` is one row per **(seller SKU, calendar month)**, summed over
**every marketplace** (`country_id`) of the single shop that has finance rows
(`finance_by_products` holds exactly one `shop_id`). `n_countries` says how many
marketplaces contributed, so a worldwide total is never mistaken for a US one.
The US-only columns exist because ~95% of the complaint records are US reviews;
they make a same-market check possible without a second table.

This is the *only* correct way to sum it: the same SKU appears on several
`products` rows (one per marketplace), each with its own finance rows, so a SKU
total is the sum across them. A SKU-month repeated per country is not a duplicate.

### What NULL means — and why it is never 0

Every ratio in the view is `n * 100 / NULLIF(denominator, 0)`. NULL therefore means
**"no denominator, so no rate"**, in exactly three cases:

1. **no sales row** for that SKU-month (13 rows today) — the product has no finance
   entry, usually a bundle;
2. **0 orders** in a month that does have a finance row (2 rows today; 561 such
   SKU-months exist in the table overall — inventory without sales);
3. a complaint record whose SKU/month is outside the finance feed's range.

A 0.00 would claim "no complaints per order" when the truth is "nothing to divide
by". The app prints `—` with that reason in the tooltip and writes an empty CSV
cell; PowerBI must do the same (see the measure rules below).

`has_sales` (1/0) separates case 1 from case 2 without a second join.

### `refunds` is the seller's own refund count — a sanity check, not a validation

`sales_by_sku_month.refunds` is `SUM(finance_by_products.refunds)` for that
SKU-month, i.e. the seller's own refund figure, and `refund_rate_pct` is
`refunds / orders`. It is on the same scale as the complaint counts, so it is the
honest cross-check for "did returns rise"; it is **not** a validation of the
complaint labels, because a refund and a review are different events and most
refunds never produce a review. The seller's own `refunds_percent` column agrees
with `refunds/orders` (July 2026: 8.98% mean of the column vs 9.01% computed), so
the two are the same measure — quoted only as a consistency check.

**Read the two refund ratios with care.** The feed only populates `refunds` for
**US and CA**: in 2026 refunds/orders is 8.4% in the US and 9.5% in Canada but
~0.2% in every EU marketplace. So `refund_rate_pct` divides a mostly-US numerator
by a **worldwide** denominator — it is comparable across months only while the
market mix is stable, and it is not a level you can quote as "our return rate".
`refund_rate_us_pct` (`refunds_us / orders_us`) is the same-market figure and the
one to quote. Two more limits: refunds lag orders, so a low-volume SKU-month can
show a rate above 100% (`SC-XL-B`, July 2026: 54 refunds on 2 orders) — read those
at family level, never per SKU-month; and `orders`/`units` are Amazon orders while
the complaints are review records, so neither ratio is a review rate.

### What this still cannot say

- The numerator is **complaint records**, and how many reviews get harvested in a
  month varies with the feed (`fba` produced 310 seat-cushion records in July,
  191 in August, 468 in September, on flat order volume). A rate that moves with
  the harvest is not a rate that moved with customers — say "complaint records per
  100 orders", never "return rate".
- `orders` is Amazon orders across marketplaces; the complaints are mostly US
  reviews. The US columns are the same-market check, not a separate model.
- A month in progress (this month) has a partial numerator and denominator; the
  app excludes today from its per-day rates and the finance feed is daily.

### How to model it in PowerBI

1. Import `v_complaint_rates` (it is a superset of `v_ai_complaints_monthly`, so
   one import replaces the old table; the counts are identical — asserted on every
   refresh).
2. **Never** average `complaints_per_100_orders`. It is a per-complaint-code rate:
   `SUM(orders)` over a SKU-month's rows counts the same orders once per code, so
   summing or averaging the rate is wrong by the number of codes on that row.
3. Build measures instead:
   - `Orders = SUMX(VALUES(v_complaint_rates[sku], v_complaint_rates[month]), MAX(v_complaint_rates[orders]))`
   - `Records = SUM(v_complaint_rates[n_complaints])`
   - `Records per 100 orders = DIVIDE([Records] * 100, [Orders])` — blank when the
     denominator is blank, which is the NULL rule holding in the model.
4. Put `title_short` on the axis and `sku` in the tooltip (some SKUs are numbers).
5. `refund_rate_pct` is a SKU-month measure too: aggregate it as
   `SUMX(VALUES(...), MAX(refunds)) / [Orders]`, never as an average of the column.
